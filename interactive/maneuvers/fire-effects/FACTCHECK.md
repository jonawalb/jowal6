# Suppress, Neutralize, Destroy: fact check

VERIFIED: 2026-10-14, independent check against the FM 3-09 and ATP 3-21.8 PDFs. Two lede sentences the checker found unsourced were reworded to the sources ("A call for fire is a standardized request for the fire needed on a target" from FM 3-09 para 4-71; "In this model each step costs..." marks the notional values).

## Independent check, 2026-10-14 (fresh download of both PDFs from armypubs.army.mil)

VERIFIED verbatim: FM 3-09 para 3-66 (pp. 3-25/3-26) suppression, neutralization, destruction definitions and percentages ("at least 3-percent" kept for suppression only), suppressive fire "during the conduct of the fires"; para 2-88 (mortars as sections in infantry BCT rifle companies; "high-angle, relatively short-range, high rate-of-fire, area fire weapons"); para 2-40 (company FIST); ATP 3-21.8 para 1-9 and para 3-46.

Open problems:
1. UNSOURCED: lede "the call names the effect wanted". FM 3-09 only says a call for fire is "a standardized request for fire containing data necessary for obtaining the required fire on a target" (para 4-71) and that destruction fire is "an element of the method of engagement portion of the call for fire" (para 3-66). TC 3-09.81 not opened. Safe rewording: "When a leader calls for fire, the effect wanted shapes what is asked for." or "A call for fire is a standardized request for the fire needed on a target."
2. UNSOURCED as stated: lede "Each step costs more rounds and more minutes" is the model's notional values, not doctrine. Reword: "In this model each step costs more rounds and more minutes."
3. Squad column with fire teams in wedge is supported (ATP 3-21.8 fig. 3-4, "Squad column"); the bounding behavior is the drawing's own choice, not cited.

Every claim below was read by the builder in the primary text on 2026-10-14. Nothing here has been independently verified.
