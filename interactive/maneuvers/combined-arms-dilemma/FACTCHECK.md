Points for the checker:
- Claim 3's mapping onto the bench (key A = automatic weapon, key B = grenade launcher, guard on A = the enemy staying down, guard on B = the enemy moving) is the page's own gloss, introduced with “On the bench … can stand for”. Check it is a fair reading of the passage.
- Row 5c lower-cases "Ground" mid-sentence.
- The brief mentioned a mortar example; the 1997 MCDP 1 uses the automatic weapon and grenade launcher, so the page uses that. No mortar claim is made.

Not factual claims (notional, labeled on the page and in METHOD.md): the lock and its geometry, the guard's hold threshold (last fifth of its travel), key turn rate (180°/s), guard crossing time (about 1.4 s), and the statement that no guard position holds both keys (a fact about the bench's geometry).

Points for the checker:
- The page maps MCDP 1's Marine example onto the Army fire team drawn by the terrain kit, and says so ("as the Army's platoon manual lists them"). The 1997 Marine fire team is not described on the page; no Marine composition claim is made.
- The enemy's three responses (stay down, rise to fire back, run back across the open) gloss MCDP 1's "pin an enemy down" and "if he moves to escape the impact of the grenades"; "rise to fire back" is the page's own addition for the guard's middle, labeled as what the drawing shows, not quoted.

Notional, not claims (labeled so on the page): figure scale (about 1.5× the ground), every size, range and timing, the terrain (hedge, wall, wood), the grenadier being held back with one arm, the enemy's escape route, and the casualty counts ("2 vs 0 down" with one arm against a guard that covers it, "1 vs 1 down" otherwise).
