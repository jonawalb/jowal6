How good is the evidence? Every rule, case and estimate links to its source. Charges are allegations unless a court has convicted. Each capacity estimate carries an evidence grade, and you choose which grades count.

How to read it

Capacity estimates. How many AI chips can China make, and how many U.S. chips reach it? Each metric shows every published estimate as a bar on a log scale, with a letter grade for the kind of evidence behind it. Switch grades on and off: the shaded envelope and the readout show the range you get if you trust only those sources.

Entity List. Each bar is one Federal Register rule that added PRC entities tied to AI chips, supercomputing, chipmaking or diversion to those end users, counted from the rule's own table. Filter by sector and click a bar for the entities and the rule's reasons.

Cases. Public DOJ and BIS cases on smuggling or diverting advanced chips and chipmaking tools, with the route alleged and the status of each case on the date checked.

Timeline. All three on one axis, with the rule changes that set the controls.

Limits in brief

Nobody outside Huawei, SMIC and a few governments knows China's real output. Many figures circulate from paywalled reports and unnamed sources; this tool cites the public statement that carries each one and grades it on that basis. A grade describes the kind of source, not whether the number is right: official figures can rest on analyst inputs, and press reports can turn out correct. Entity List counts are entries in the rule's table under the China destination, which includes Hong Kong addresses. The full method is under Method & sources.
