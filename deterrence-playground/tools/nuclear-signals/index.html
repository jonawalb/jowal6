    How to read it

    States signal about nuclear weapons in four ways: what their leaders and ministries say, what their forces do (tests, drills, deployments), what they write into doctrine, and what they do with arms-control treaties. The Observatory puts all four on one timeline for the nine nuclear-armed states.

    Timeline. One lane per state. Filter by category, state and period, or search. Click a mark or a row to read the item, its sources and its coding in the side panel. Arrow keys step through items once one is selected.

    State dossier. One state at a time: warhead estimates, nuclear tests, declared policy, treaty status, where its dyads sit on the ladder, and its signals since 2021.

    Escalation ladder. Each pair of nuclear-armed rivals is placed on Herman Kahn's 44-rung ladder at the highest rung of any placed item in the chosen window. Move the date, change the window, and read the evidence list: every item says which rule set its rung.

    Rhetoric vs events. Month by month, how much official text talks about nuclear weapons and how threatening a tone model scores it, with the same state's events on top. Click a month to read the sentences behind the numbers.

    Limits in brief

    Rhetoric series exist only for states in the rhetoric corpus: Russia, China, the United States, India and Pakistan (and Iran, which is not nuclear-armed). Missile tests and drills since 2021 are complete for Russia and a verified selection for the others. A rung is an ordinal label for a kind of move, not a probability of war. The full method, coverage gaps and every source are under Method & sources.

    Built from the data of Russia's Nuclear Signals, Who Promises What, Treaty Tracker, Every Nuclear Test, Nuclear Arsenals, Kahn's Escalation Ladder, Is It a Nuke? and Nuclear Entanglement, plus events verified for this tool and the rhetoric corpus.
