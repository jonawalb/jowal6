    How the exercise works
    • The attacker follows a sequence of steps drawn from public reporting on its type of group. Each turn it tries its next step or two. Your controls change its odds; your telemetry decides whether you see it. You see alerts, not the attacker.
    • Footholds. Each successful step can leave access behind: an account, a host, privileged credentials, persistence, cloud tokens, a mailbox rule. Remove some while others survive and the attacker notices (a "tip-off"): some groups speed up, some dig in, insiders grab what they can.
    • Alerts include false positives. Triage tells them apart. Containment hits every open alert of its kind, so acting before triage locks out innocent employees.
    • Seats. CEO, CISO, IT/SecOps, Legal and Comms each get a few actions a turn. Business-disrupting containment needs the CEO's authorization. Declaring a major incident gives every seat more capacity; outside responders add hands to IT.
    • Clocks start when the rule says they do (awareness, a materiality determination, operational disruption, a confirmed data breach, a ransom payment) and run in real hours against the game clock.
    • Score: attacker denied 35%, operations 20%, cost net of insurance 15%, obligations met 15%, reputation 10%, evidence 5%.
    Running it as a tabletop
    • Project the screen. Seat at least one person per role: CEO, CISO, IT/SecOps, Legal, Comms. Add HR, finance or operations as observers if you like.
    • Each turn: read the new information aloud, give each seat its question, set the timer (8–10 minutes), let each seat choose its actions, then end the turn. Six to eight turns take 60–90 minutes.
    • The facilitator can peek at the attacker's real position to steer the discussion. Do not show the room.
    • Turn on Discussion first so the room argues before it sees the menu, and Coaching hints off so the options do not give the answers away.
    • Throw a curveball when the room gets comfortable: the CEO on a plane, a reporter with the story, a deepfake call to Finance. Each resolves on what the room does that turn.
    • Type the room's arguments into the notes box; they go into the after-action report. Finish with a hotwash, rate the objectives, assign owners and dates in the improvement plan, then export the report.
