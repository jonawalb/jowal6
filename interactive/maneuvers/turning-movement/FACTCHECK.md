# Turning Movement: fact check

**VERIFIED 2026-10-13 by an independent fact-checker (FM 3-90, 1 May 2023, Wayback copy; paras 2-71, 2-72, 2-73, 2-80). All quotes are exact. Fixed after the check: the lede and meta give both branches of the 2-80 dilemma and say "objective in its rear" instead of "vital point"; the four elements of 2-72 are listed; item 5 is marked "in this model".**

Source opened this session: Headquarters, Department of the Army, FM 3-90, *Tactics*, 1 May 2023 (supersedes FM 3-90-1 and FM 3-90-2, 22 March 2013).
Official URL: https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN38160-FM_3-90-000-WEB-1.pdf
armypubs.army.mil did not resolve from this machine and the FAS mirror (irp.fas.org/doddir/army/fm3-90.pdf) returned an empty 202, so the text was read from the Wayback Machine copy of the official PDF: https://web.archive.org/web/20260804103454/https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN38160-FM_3-90-000-WEB-1.pdf (title page: "MAY 2023", "Washington, D.C., 01 May 2023").

| # | Claim on the page | Where | Source location |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A turning movement is a form of maneuver "in which the attacking force seeks to avoid the enemy's principal defensive positions by attacking to the rear of their current positions forcing them to move or divert forces to meet the threat" | Card caption; "What doctrine says" | Para 2-71, p. 2-22 (same wording in the Glossary entry "turning movement") |
| 2 | It differs from an envelopment because the turning force "seeks to make enemy forces displace from their current locations, whereas an enveloping force seeks to engage enemy forces in their current locations from an unexpected direction" | "What doctrine says" | Para 2-71, p. 2-22 |
| 3 | The turning force is the main effort; the diversion force's attacks are "designed to divert the enemy force's attention away from the turning force" | "What doctrine says"; "How to read it" item 2 | Para 2-73, p. 2-23 (organization listed in para 2-72) |
| 4 | Enemy forces "must move from their original positions to meet the threat presented by the turning force," or "continue to hold the terrain they occupy at the risk of becoming enveloped and potentially encircled" | "What doctrine says" | Para 2-80, p. 2-24 |

Not doctrinal claims: the trigger share, guard rate, carriage speeds, lever swing and bolt travel are notional and labeled so on the page ("What is notional") and in METHOD.md. The bench's equation of "lever thrown, bolt drawn" with the "hold" branch of the dilemma is the model's picture, not doctrine's wording.
