Read with care. These are thin, noisy markets. A correlation says two lines moved together in this window. It does not show that PLA activity moved the market, or the reverse.

What a market price is

Each contract here is a Polymarket share that pays $1 if a stated event happens by a deadline and nothing if it does not. The price is what the last buyer and seller agreed to swap it for. A price of 4¢ is often read as “a 4% chance,” and that is a reasonable first reading, but it is a price. It comes from whoever chose to put money in, it reflects the cost of tying up cash until the deadline, and on quiet days it can rest on a handful of small trades.

It is not a forecast from analysts. No intelligence service, think tank or survey of experts sets these numbers. That is the point of the comparison: traders on one side, and on the other the record analysts watch, TSM’s daily count of PLA aircraft and the major exercises around Taiwan.

How to read the chart

  • Lines are daily “Yes” prices in cents. Several contracts on the same event with different deadlines form a ladder; later deadlines cost more, and each line falls toward zero as its deadline passes quietly.
  • The grey line is PLA aircraft reported around Taiwan, as a 7-day mean, on the right axis. Shaded bands are major exercises and ticks are joint combat readiness patrols.
  • The volume strip shows dollars traded each day in the focus market. When it is nearly empty, a single trade sets the price.
  • The lead/lag chart shifts the aircraft series against the price and reports the correlation at each lag. The shaded band is the rough range for pure noise.

What is missing

There is no matching daily series of expert forecasts, so the “analysts” side of this page is the activity record analysts use, not their probability judgments. The market archive is incomplete for some older contracts: for the 2024 and 2025 invasion contracts, only the last weeks of trading survive, because Polymarket stops serving price history once a contract resolves. Those gaps are shown as gaps. Kalshi contracts are not included.

Method

  • Daily price: the last hourly price bar of the day (UTC) where the archive has bars, which is from July 2026 on; otherwise the size-weighted median of that day’s fills, counting a “No” fill at price p as a “Yes” price of 1 − p. Days with no trades are left blank, never filled in.
  • Daily volume: the sum of shares times price across the day’s fills.
  • Aircraft 7-day mean: the average of the reported days in the trailing seven, shown only when at least four days were reported.
  • Lead/lag: Pearson correlation of aircraft at day t with price at day t + k, for every k in the chosen range, over matched days in the window. The noise band is ±1.96/√n. It ignores autocorrelation and the number of lags scanned, so it is generous to finding a pattern.
  • Left out: “Will China invade Taiwan before GTA VI?”, which paid 50-50 if neither event happened, so its price is not a probability of invasion.
