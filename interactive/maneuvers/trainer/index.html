Maneuver Trainer

The figures in this section explain the ideas. The trainer asks you to use them. Each scenario gives you an objective, a piece of ground and a force, and you decide how to maneuver: who fixes the enemy, who moves, by which route and when. Then the enemy shoots back.

Three games, one set of scenarios

Play

Easy

Plan, then watch: pick a scheme of maneuver, set each element's task, route and trigger, and see it play out.

Play

Medium

Turn-based orders: give every element its orders, run one turn, see what changed, order again.

Play

Hard

Real time: the clock runs while you give orders, and at company level the radio delays them.

Every game uses the same twelve scenarios and the same simulation, and scores the same way: the objective first, then your losses, then time, followed by an after-action review that names the concepts you used, the traps you avoided and the ideas you missed, each linked to its figure.

What this is, and is not

The scenarios, place names and enemy are fictional, and every number in the simulation (ranges, rates of fire, suppression, casualties, times) is a notional teaching value, not data. Casualties fall on both sides even when a plan works, shown greyed and still, never graphic. This is a teaching game, not a US Army training aid.

Doctrine terms follow Headquarters, Department of the Army, ATP 3-21.8, Infantry Rifle Platoon and Squad (11 January 2024, incorporating Change 1, June 2025), paras. 1-9, 1-11 and 3-56, and FM 3-90, Tactics (1 May 2023), para. 2-24.
