How to read it

Declaratory policy is what a government says, in an official document, about when it would or would not use nuclear weapons. It is not the same as war plans, which no state publishes, and a promise can be withdrawn. The matrix records the words and their dates, and leaves judgment about credibility to the reader.

The five columns are: no first use (a pledge never to be the first to use nuclear weapons, or an explicit refusal to make one); negative security assurances (promises not to use nuclear weapons against states without them); role (sole purpose, fundamental role or similar statements of what the weapons are for); conditions for use; and alert posture (official statements about readiness). The short label on each cell is a finding aid; the quote is the evidence.

Move the year slider or drag the timeline to see what was on record at an earlier date. A cell reads "Not declared" when no official statement on that element was found up to that year.

Method

  • Every quote was copied from the official text and checked word for word against a saved copy of the source on 2026-09-29, after normalizing spaces, curly quotes and dashes. "…" marks a skip or the join between two passages of one document. Official English translations are used for Russia, China, France and North Korea; North Korean texts are KCNA's English versions from the kcna.co.jp mirror.
  • A cell shows the latest formal document on each element dated on or before the chosen year. Later statements that comment on a doctrine without replacing it, such as India's Defence Minister in 2019, appear in the cell's history in the panel. Superseded documents stay in the history too.
  • "Not declared" means no official statement on that element was found in the documents listed in the panel note. "None quoted before" means this tool quotes nothing earlier, although earlier documents may exist; the timeline lists some that are not quoted. Nothing is inferred from silence.
  • The short labels are this tool's finding aids. Where a label could be read as a pledge, the quote decides: Israel's "not the first to introduce" formula, for example, is not a no-first-use pledge, and Pakistan's 2016 statement backs a treaty on assurances without giving one.
  • Some official sites refuse automated requests (media.defense.gov, mid.ru, mea.gov.in text, ispr.gov.pk); archived copies are linked where they were used. Pakistan's military press releases, where National Command Authority statements appear, could not be reached, so Pakistan's row may be incomplete. Israel's government sites were not searched; the Israel entries come from U.S. records in Foreign Relations of the United States.
  • Data: data/policies.js, built by scripts/build_data.py from the verified records in scripts/verified/.
