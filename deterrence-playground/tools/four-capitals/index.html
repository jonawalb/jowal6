Choose your capital

March 2029. Beijing is turning up the pressure on Taiwan. Over eight months, each capital picks a posture and three moves a month, all at once. Every capital has a hidden type (resolute, cautious or opportunist) that the others have to read from its behavior.

Weight each goal from 0 (ignore) to 5 (vital). You are scored against these weights at the end, and compared with the computer playing your seat for the same goals.

      How the game works
      • Each month you pick a posture (stand down to escalate; the United States and China can also make a nuclear signal from Blockade up) and up to three strategic moves across seven lines: diplomatic, information, military, economic, financial, intelligence and law enforcement. Each move asks follow-up questions: which route, which ports, which sector, which coast. Some moves unlock as the crisis climbs; opportunity moves (●) appear only when conditions allow. All four capitals reveal at once.
      • Forces. Every capital has force points in four sea areas (North, Strait, South, East) and its Rear. Spend your monthly logistics to move them and set each area to Defend, Contest or Attack. Holding areas matters: a blockade needs China to hold the Strait and a flank, escorts need their route, a landing needs the Strait. Below Limited strikes nobody fires; after that, areas where both sides contest fight every month. You see rivals’ strength only roughly (~) unless your intelligence moves paid off.
      • Every move shows its odds and the factors behind them. One roll decides it: success, partial or failure. Military moves need the crisis to have reached a certain rung.
      • The ladder runs from Pressure to Nuclear use. It climbs when escalatory moves succeed, at most two rungs a month, and eases when capitals step back.
      • Hidden types. Resolute leaders pay at home for backing down; cautious ones pay for escalating; opportunists escalate when a rival looks weak. The computer updates its beliefs about you by Bayes' rule from what you do, and costly moves say the most.
      • Nuclear use is never a menu choice. Once the shooting starts, a rising nuclear shadow makes it a risk each month.
      • Endings: a settlement after two calm months of talks; Beijing stepping back; Taiwan forced to terms; nuclear use; or October.

      All numbers are illustrative game design. In 1,000 test games with the computer in every seat on Normal: 10% ended with Taiwan forced to terms, 21% with Beijing stepping back, 2% in a settlement, 5% in nuclear use, and 63% were unresolved in October. 58% saw a blockade or worse, 32% limited strikes or worse, and 10% major war or nuclear use. By month 5 the computer’s best guess of a rival’s type was right 67% of the time (chance is 33%).
