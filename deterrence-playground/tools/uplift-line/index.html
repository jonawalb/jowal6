Where's the Uplift Line?

A triage trainer for conventional-weapons content decisions. Read an abstracted request, make a call, then compare it with a reference call and the reasoning behind it.

Training exercise. Reference calls are one analyst's judgment, written for practice. They are not any company's policy. Scenarios describe requests at category level only and contain no operational detail.

The rubric

The question is not "is this about weapons?" but "would answering this give someone a meaningful step toward causing harm that they could not easily take otherwise?" Score each factor from 0 (no concern) to 4 (high concern). No single factor decides a case; a high intent score can override low scores elsewhere, and public availability does not excuse a response that assembles scattered pieces into a working procedure.

Aggregation across turns

Judge the conversation, not the last message. A sequence of individually harmless turns can add up to a procedure, and a stated purpose early in a conversation carries forward. Ask: if all the answers in this thread were printed on one page, what would that page let someone do? The reverse also holds: alarming keywords across turns are not aggregation if the turns add up to a novel, a lesson plan or a policy paper.

The five calls

Answer fully
Legitimate request, no meaningful uplift. Refusing is an over-refusal.
Answer with safety framing
Answer, and make safety, law or harm reduction part of the content. Not a lecture bolted on.
Answer general level only, decline specifics
The topic is legitimate but part of the request crosses from principle into procedure. Give the principle and say what you are leaving out.
Decline
The core of the request is uplift or the intent is harmful. Offer a safe adjacent answer where one exists.
Escalate to specialist
Decline and route to a threat or specialist review process: a specific target, credible threat, or a case outside the reviewer's competence.

Scoring in this trainer

The calls are ordered from least to most restrictive. An exact match earns 2 points and a call one step away earns 1. An over-refusal (false positive) is a Decline or Escalate where the reference answers in some form. An under-refusal (false negative) is Answer fully or Answer with safety framing where the reference declines or escalates. The calibration view shows which way you lean.

Method & sources

What the data is

Forty hand-written, abstracted scenarios in data/scenarios.json. Each describes a request to an AI assistant at category level, never the dangerous content itself and never a plausible answer. About 55% are requests that should be answered in some form, because over-refusal is the more common failure in practice and the harder one to train out. The rest are gray-zone cases and clear violations, including multi-turn trajectories and pairs where the same question arrives with a different stated purpose.

How it was built

Each reference call, its factor scores and its rationale are set against the five-factor rubric. Where a rationale cites law, it links to the primary text, and each citation was checked against that text. scripts/validate.py checks the file for missing fields, bad scores and broken source links. Your answers stay in this browser.

Limits

  • Reference calls are one analyst's judgment for training. Reasonable reviewers will disagree on gray-zone cases, and that disagreement is part of the exercise.
  • Legal references are U.S. federal and summarized for context. They are not legal advice, and state law varies.
  • Abstracted scenarios remove the wording cues that make real cases hard. Real review also weighs platform, account history and prior turns that a one-paragraph scenario cannot carry.
