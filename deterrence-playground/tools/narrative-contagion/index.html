Read this first

  • A shared framing is not proof of coordination. Most clusters are one news event reported by outlets in several countries, or one side quoting the other. Official-to-official matches are the likelier cases of a repeated line, and even those include joint statements and readouts of the same meeting.
  • “First seen” means first in this corpus, not where the framing started. Outlets publish on different schedules and the corpus misses many sources.
  • Coverage differs by country and by month. China's state-media article text covers only April to June 2026, so most clusters fall in 2026. Check the coverage strip before reading an absence as silence.

    What a cluster is

    Passages from different countries whose meaning, measured by a multilingual sentence model, is nearly identical (cosine similarity at least 0.93) and dated within 14 days of each other. Chains of such matches form one cluster, so its first and last items can be further apart.

    How to read it

    Network. Each node is a country, or with Outlets each ministry, presidency or media outlet. A link joins two nodes that appear in the same cluster; its width counts those clusters. An arrow points away from the side that appeared first in at least 60% of the clusters where the two were not on the same day. Press Play to build the network month by month; links new in a month turn orange. Click a node to filter to a country, or a link to list the clusters behind it.

    Who echoes whom. The matrix counts, for each pair of countries, how often the row country appeared before the column country. Switch to Net lead to see which side is more often first, or to Median lag to see how many days separate them.

    Official vs media. The four tiles split all dated passage matches by the kind of source on each side, with the lag between them.

    Dossier. Selecting a framing lists every item in date order with its link, the item each one matched most closely, and the corpus coverage for that month. Play the spread replays the cluster on the network, day by day, from the first-seen source outward.

    Method & sources

    • Corpus. Walberg's multilingual rhetoric corpus (the same collection behind Rhetoric Search): official statements, briefings and transcripts from foreign ministries, presidencies, parliaments and defense ministries, and state media, for China, Russia, Iran, Belarus, Türkiye, Syria, Pakistan, India, Taiwan, the United States, Venezuela and Cuba. Each source's collection method, dates and gaps are documented in the corpus's SOURCES.md. Built on 2026-10-04.
    • Matching. Each document contributes up to four passages of about 200 tokens plus its title (titles only for articles and headlines; official titles are formulaic, and per-source title templates are dropped). Passages under 60 characters (24 in Chinese, Japanese or Korean) are skipped. Passages are embedded with intfloat/multilingual-e5-small, so matches cross languages. 712,176 passages were compared; pairs from different countries dated within 14 days with cosine similarity of at least 0.93 form 5,162 matches, and connected groups of matches form 2,248 clusters. The threshold was set by reading sampled pairs per similarity band: at 0.93 and above most pairs are the same event, statement or joint text.
    • First seen, lead and lag. Dates are publication dates as collected (some are listing dates a day late). A country's position in a cluster is the date of its first item. Same-day pairs have no order and are counted separately. Lags are whole days.
    • Topics. Each document has a topic from an unsupervised model fitted on the whole corpus (50 topics, version d87bc5e3fd). A cluster takes its members' most common topic. Topic names are the model's top English terms, not hand labels.
    • Official vs media. As classified in the corpus. Taiwan's CNA and Focus Taiwan are state-owned and classed as state media; the Taipei Times is the only independent outlet.
    • Text policy. Official passages are shown up to 300 characters with a link. Media items show headline and link only. The build script refuses to write the data file if any media body sentence would ship.
    • Limits. The model measures shared meaning, so two outlets summarising one event in similar words will match. Matches across a 14-day window miss slower echoes. Coverage is uneven: China's state-media article text covers April to June 2026 (headlines January 2025 to June 2026), Russian state media is a dated sample, several official sources come partly from the Wayback Machine, and North Korea is missing. Clusters and the rankings that depend on them are descriptive leads, not findings.
    • How to cite. Jonathan Walberg, Narrative Contagion Network, Interactive Deterrence (jwalberg.com), data built 2026-10-04, cluster id as shown in the dossier.
