# Main Effort and Economy of Force: fact check

VERIFIED 2026-10-13 by an independent fact-checker (ADP 3-0 2019 paras 4-38, 4-39; FM 3-0 2022 table 1-1, para A-10; JP 3-0 2017 Ch1 app. A 2e(1)). All quotes verbatim, qualifiers kept. Fixed after the check: the lede now uses the source terms ("favorable correlation of forces", "preponderance", "minimum necessary") and attributes each half to its manual; the ADP link points to the archived copy, because the FAS mirror serves a bot challenge.

| Claim on the page | Where | Source text location |
|---|---|---|
| A commander "seldom" has enough combat power to be stronger everywhere (lede paraphrase) | Lede | FM 3-0 (2022), para A-10, p. A-2: "there is seldom enough combat power available to enjoy a favorable correlation of forces everywhere" |
| Doctrine names one main effort, usually gives it most of the combat power, holds the rest with the least that will do (lede paraphrase) | Lede | ADP 3-0 (2019), paras 4-38 and 4-39, p. 4-6 |
| Main effort: "a designated subordinate unit whose mission at a given point in time is most critical to overall mission success. It is usually weighted with the preponderance of combat power. Typically, commanders shift the main effort one or more times during execution" | Caption (part), doctrine section | ADP 3-0 (2019), para 4-38, p. 4-6 (definition also in glossary) |
| Supporting effort: "a designated subordinate unit with a mission that supports the success of the main effort"; commanders "resource supporting efforts with the minimum assets necessary to accomplish the mission" | Doctrine section | ADP 3-0 (2019), para 4-39, p. 4-6 |
| Economy of force is among the nine principles of war | Doctrine section | FM 3-0 (2022), para 1-33 and table 1-1, pp. 1-7 to 1-8; para A-3 |
| Economy of force: "Expend minimum-essential combat power on secondary efforts to allocate the maximum possible combat power on the main effort." | Caption, doctrine section | FM 3-0 (2022), table 1-1, p. 1-8 |
| Mass: "Concentrate the effects of combat power at the most advantageous place and time to produce decisive results." | Doctrine section | FM 3-0 (2022), table 1-1, p. 1-8 |
| Economy of force is "a reciprocal of mass" that "requires deliberately accepting risk in selected areas to achieve relative superiority—or overwhelming effects—in others" | Doctrine section | FM 3-0 (2022), para A-10, pp. A-2 to A-3 |
| Joint doctrine uses nearly the same words, with "primary efforts" in place of "the main effort" | Doctrine section | JP 3-0 (2017, Ch 1 2018), appendix A, para 2e(1), p. A-2: "The purpose of economy of force is to expend minimum essential combat power on secondary efforts to allocate the maximum possible combat power on primary efforts." |

Not doctrinal claims: the 12 discs, the breach at 6, the hold at 1, gate travel and loader speeds are notional and labeled so on the page and in METHOD.md. "The loader stands for that shift" and "breach / held / exposed" are statements about the bench model.
