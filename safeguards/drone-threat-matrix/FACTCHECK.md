# FACTCHECK: Non-State Drone Misuse Matrix

Compiled 2026-10-06. Every source URL below was opened with a web fetch during compilation and checked against the claim. Sources that could not be opened (paywalls, 403s, an unreadable UN PDF) were removed, and cases left with one source were recoded `single-source`. A script re-check (`scripts/validate_data.py --check-urls`) returned no 404s; 13 URLs return 403 or time out to scripted requests (bot blocking) but loaded in the compilation fetch.

Descriptions are category level by design. Operational detail present in some sources (payloads, models, methods) was deliberately left out.

## Actor profiles

- **Islamic State (Iraq and Syria)**: The Islamic State ran the most extensive non-state drone programme of 2014-2017. It moved from aerial reconnaissance and propaganda filming in Syria in 2014 to a managed drone unit under its military manufacturing committee, then to armed commercial quadcopters used at scale around Mosul. Use peaked in spring 2017 and fell as the group lost territory; since 2024 supporter networks have circulated drone guidance online.
  - Combating Terrorism Center at West Point: https://ctc.westpoint.edu/islamic-state-drones-supply-scale-future-threats/ (verified: y)
  - Combating Terrorism Center at West Point: https://ctc.westpoint.edu/ctc-perspectives-the-islamic-states-drone-documents-management-acquisitions-and-diy-tradecraft (verified: y)
- **Hamas (Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades)**: Hamas's armed wing has run an in-house drone programme for years, which it credits to engineer Mohamed Zouari, killed in 2016. It showed reconnaissance drones in 2014 and one-way attack drones in 2021. On 7 October 2023 it used small drones at the opening of a combined assault, which researchers describe as a watershed in non-state drone use. Sponsor note: Hamas receives Iranian support, but the cases here concern drones it says it produced itself; it is not flagged as state-backed in the filters.
  - ICCT / Perspectives on Terrorism: https://pt.icct.nl/article/drones-and-hamas-led-attack-7-october-2023-innovation-and-implications (verified: y)
  - Al Majalla: https://en.majalla.com/node/301776 (verified: y)
- **Houthis (Ansar Allah)**: Ansar Allah controls much of northern Yemen and has used one-way attack drones against Yemeni government leaders, Saudi and Emirati infrastructure and airports, Red Sea shipping and Israel. Its claims are sometimes contested: UN monitors rejected its claim to the 2019 Abqaiq attack. Sponsor note: The UN Panel of Experts on Yemen documents Iranian-supplied drone systems and technology combined with commercially sourced parts. Its capabilities are not representative of unsupported groups.
  - Washington Institute for Near East Policy: https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/un-exposes-houthi-reliance-iranian-weapons (verified: y)
  - Bellingcat: https://www.bellingcat.com/news/mena/2019/01/10/houthis-use-armed-drone-to-target-yemeni-army-top-brass (verified: y)
- **Hezbollah**: Hezbollah has flown drones into Israeli airspace since 2004. In the 2024 war its attack drones killed four soldiers at a training base near Binyamina and hit the prime minister's private home in Caesarea. Sponsor note: Researchers describe its drones as supplied by Iran. Included for comparison; its capabilities sit closer to a state military than to most groups here.
  - Federation of American Scientists: https://fas.org/publication/hezbollah-use-drones-weapon-terrorism/ (verified: y)
- **Mexican cartels**: Mexican criminal groups have used drones to move drugs across the US border since at least 2015. Armed use appeared in 2017-18 and grew quickly from 2020, led by the CJNG, which fields a dedicated drone unit; CSIS cites 5 attacks in 2020 rising to 260 in the first half of 2023. La Nueva Familia Michoacana, Los Viagras and warring Sinaloa factions have since adopted the tactic, mostly against rivals, self-defence groups and rural communities.
  - CSIS: https://www.csis.org/analysis/illicit-innovation-latin-america-not-prepared-fight-criminal-drones (verified: y)
  - Cronkite News: https://cronkitenews.azpbs.org/2024/03/28/senators-told-of-alarming-level-of-drone-incursions-at-southern-border/ (verified: y)
- **Colombian armed groups (FARC dissidents, ELN)**: FARC dissident fronts, especially those of the EMC under Iván Mordisco, led drone attacks in Colombia; the ELN and Clan del Golfo followed. Most attacks target military and police in Cauca, Catatumbo and Antioquia, but civilian casualties are rising. ACLED counts rose from 1 attack in 2023 to 38 in 2024 and 149 in 2025; Ministry of Defence counts are higher.
  - Al Jazeera: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/longform/2026/5/22/i-always-hear-them-before-i-see-them-drones-strike-fear-in-colombia (verified: y)
  - ACLED: https://acleddata.com/media-citation/drones-reshape-war-colombia-deaths-and-injuries-mount-guardian (verified: y)
- **Sahel and Lake Chad jihadist groups (JNIM, IS Sahel, ISWAP)**: The al-Qaeda affiliate JNIM began armed drone use in Mali in September 2023 and has since extended it to Burkina Faso and Togo. Islamic State affiliates followed: ISWAP in north-east Nigeria from December 2024 and Islamic State Sahel in Niger in 2025. Reporting describes reliance on commercial quadcopters.
  - ACLED: https://acleddata.com/2025/04/04/africa-overview-april-2025 (verified: y)
  - Lawfare: https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/african-non-state-actors-put-drones-on-the-attack (verified: y)
- **Somali jihadist groups (al-Shabaab, IS Somalia)**: Al-Shabaab has mainly used drones for propaganda and surveillance, and UN monitors report it sought weapons and training from the Houthis in 2024. Islamic State Somalia carried out its first recorded drone strikes in January 2025 against Puntland forces.
  - ISS Africa: https://issafrica.org/iss-today/houthis-in-somalia-friends-with-technological-benefits (verified: y)
- **Myanmar resistance forces (PDFs, ethnic armed organisations)**: After the 2021 coup, People's Defence Forces under the National Unity Government and allied ethnic armed organisations built drone units from commercial and locally made aircraft, in effect their only air force. ACLED records more than 2,100 resistance drone strike events since December 2021.
  - ACLED: https://acleddata.com/report/war-sky-how-drone-warfare-shaping-conflict-myanmar (verified: y)
  - Myanmar Now: https://myanmar-now.org/en/news/myanmars-resistance-air-force-flies-high-in-fight-against-regime (verified: y)
- **Other non-state actors (Venezuela plot, PKK, Punjab smugglers)**: Single well-sourced cases from outside the main theatres: the unattributed 2018 drone attack at a Caracas military parade, the PKK's use of commercial drones against Turkish forces, and cross-border drone drug drops into Indian Punjab. Each case notes its attribution limits.
  - Bellingcat: https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2018/08/07/drones-attack-maduro-caracas/ (verified: y)
- **Prison contraband networks**: Organised crime groups, often working with inmates who coordinate by smuggled phone, use commercial drones to deliver drugs, phones and weapons into prisons. UK government data recorded 1,712 prison drone incidents in England and Wales in 2024-25, and the Chief Inspector of Prisons called the problem a national security threat.
  - UK Ministry of Justice: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/counter-drone-efforts-rise-as-prison-sightings-revealed (verified: y)
  - HM Inspectorate of Prisons: https://hmiprisons.justiceinspectorates.gov.uk/news/drones-dropping-drugs-and-weapons-into-high-security-prisons-are-a-threat-to-national-security (verified: y)
- **Airport disruption (unidentified operators)**: Reported drone sightings near runways have suspended flights at major hubs, including Gatwick in 2018, Heathrow, Newark and Dubai in 2019 and Frankfurt in 2020. Operators are rarely identified: the Gatwick investigation closed without charges, and many sightings are never confirmed.
  - LBC: https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/gatwick-drone-investigation-closed-by-police-DWyg8Z_2/ (verified: y)
- **Activists, individuals and unattributed overflights**: Activists have used small drones as protest tools near leaders and sensitive sites, as in Dresden in 2013, Tokyo in 2015 and at Heathrow in 2019. The cluster also holds unattributed overflights of French nuclear plants and Paris landmarks in 2014-15, and one accidental crash at the White House for contrast. Each case is labelled deliberate, unattributed or accidental.
  - Center for the Study of the Drone, Bard College: https://dronecenter.bard.edu/pirate-drone-germany (verified: y)
