Data, not a model
Question:
Does official Russian, Iranian or Venezuelan rhetoric change before those states act, or only alongside or after?
What you do:
Pick a rhetoric stream and measure, a behaviour series, and a time window.
What you will see:
A verdict from three timing tests, both series with spike weeks marked, and how many tests survive correction.
Limits:
Associations in past data, not causes or forecasts. Tone scores are machine estimates. Iranian attacks and all Venezuelan pairs are too sparse to test.

Headline finding

Across four views (6,632 tests in all, corrected within each view), 6 survive correction for multiple testing, and none since 2025. Corrected all together, 5 survive. 4 of the 6 come from one stream: Zakharova and Medvedev Telegram channels (archived copies), Russian.

Tested: Russian rhetoric against Russian strikes on Ukraine and nuclear-related actions; Iranian rhetoric against Hormuz and Red Sea shipping, which are outcomes, not Iranian actions. Not tested: Iran's own attacks on Israel (three episodes, too few) and every Venezuelan pair (too few weeks with Foreign Ministry texts before the ACLED data end in September 2024). Those series are shown for context.

Presenting this in 3 minutes
  1. Say: "Threat language about Ukraine on Zakharova's and Medvedev's Telegram channels, against Russian launches at Ukraine each week. None of the three tests reaches p < 0.05."
  2. Say: "Conciliatory sentences that mention the U.S. were followed a week later by more launches: r = +0.33, and two tests survive correction across 1,912. But the event study has only 4 spikes, and launches were already +17.75 a day above baseline before them, so any later rise may continue a climb already under way."
  3. Say: "Since 2025, nothing survives correction at either resolution. The lead-lag r here is +0.34, larger than before, but it does not count: with only 87 weeks the page judges it by a shuffle check (p 0.14), not the model p, because with so few periods the model cannot reliably tell a real link from each series' own slow drift."
  4. Say: "This switches the behaviour to drones only, where one test survives. Every spike links to what was said, in the original language, untranslated. This one is about Washington–Caracas dialogue: the measure counts any conciliatory sentence that mentions the U.S. Posts that quote an opponent are left out of this list."

Takeaway: across four views (6,632 tests, each view corrected on its own), 6 survive and none since 2025 (5 if all are corrected together), so in this record shifts in public rhetoric rarely came before shifts in what Russia did (Iran is tested only against shipping; Venezuela is not tested).

Say aloud: the weekly survivors all come from archived copies of two Telegram channels that also repost opponents' words, so treat them as leads to check, not findings.

Verdict

    p: how likely a result this strong would be if rhetoric and behaviour were unrelated. q: p adjusted for every test in this view (Benjamini–Hochberg); below 0.05 counts as surviving correction.

      How to read this

      Say. Each rhetoric series comes from one source in one language (a "stream"), such as Kremlin transcripts in Russian or the Iranian Foreign Ministry in English. A machine-learning model scores every sentence for six tones. Overall tone averages all sentences; tone on a target averages only sentences that mention Ukraine, the U.S., NATO, "the West" or Israel; attention is the share of documents that mention the target. Streams are never pooled, so a change in which outlets publish cannot look like a change in tone. A stream can still mix several speakers from the same source: the Telegram stream is Maria Zakharova's and Dmitry Medvedev's channels together, taken from archived copies of their public pages, so it holds only the posts that were captured.

      Do. Russia: missiles and drones launched at Ukraine each day, as the Ukrainian Air Force reported them, and a curated list of Russian nuclear-related actions (its three "uses" are launches of the nuclear-capable Oreshnik with non-nuclear payloads). Iran: daily ship transits through Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb (outcomes, not Iranian actions) and the days of Iran's direct attacks on Israel (too few to test). Venezuela: weekly ACLED counts of protests, riots and state violence against civilians (no pair has enough rhetoric to test).

      What kind of evidence. Empirical: the behaviour series and the quoted texts, sourced below. Model-derived: the tone and attention scores. Statistical: every r, p and q. Nothing on this page is a simulation.

      The three tests all ask whether rhetoric moves first. (1) The lead-lag chart correlates the two series' deviations from their own recent baselines at each offset; blue bars on the right are rhetoric-first. (2) The event study lines up rhetoric spike weeks and measures how behaviour changed from before the spike to after it, against randomly placed pseudo-spikes. (3) The distributed-lag regression asks whether past rhetoric adds anything once behaviour's own recent past is known.

      Verdicts. "Survives correction" means q < 0.05 after Benjamini–Hochberg across every test in the current resolution and window. "Suggestive at most" means p < 0.05 for that test alone. The four views overlap, so the page does not add their survivors as if one correction covered them all.

      What this can and cannot tell you

      Rhetoric and behaviour often rise in the same week, plausibly because both respond to the same event: a battlefield turn, a summit, a strike by the other side. Same-period correlation is shown for context but is not a test of which comes first. Even a surviving result is an association in the past record. It does not show that a statement caused an action, or that the pattern will hold next time. Coverage is uneven (see below), the tone model has been checked only against another model, and a stream's tone can shift when its spokesperson or format changes. Spokesperson channels often quote opponents in order to rebut them, and the tone model cannot tell a quoted claim from the speaker's own words: such quotes are removed from the evidence lists on this page but are still inside the tone scores. Quotes appear in the original language (Russian, Persian or Spanish) without translation. "Tone on the U.S." means sentences that mention the U.S., on any subject, so a spike in conciliation on the U.S. on a Russian channel can be about Venezuela.

      Method & sources

      • Rhetoric. Local multilingual corpus (~/Projects/rhetoric-corpus), semantic layer. Sentences are embedded with multilingual-e5-small and scored by logistic-regression students trained on labels from a zero-shot NLI teacher (bge-m3-zeroshot-v2.0). Document tone is the mean sentence probability; series are daily sums exported within each stream by scripts/say_do_export.py and aggregated to weeks (Monday start). Keyword-filtered collection regimes are excluded.
      • Anomalies. Each period's value minus the document-weighted mean of the previous 12 weeks (28 days) for rhetoric and the plain mean for behaviour. Both are standardised and winsorised at ±3 SD; periods without rhetoric count as zero deviation.
      • (a) Lead-lag. Pearson r at each offset. 95% intervals: moving-block bootstrap (block ≈ n1/3). p: Fisher z with the Pyper–Peterman effective sample size for autocorrelated series, Bonferroni over the lead lags, so one p per direction. Check: circular-shift permutation of the rhetoric series.
      • (b) Event study. Spikes from the export (rule below), declustered so no spike falls within h periods of an earlier one (their windows can still overlap). Abnormal behaviour = value minus the mean of an estimation window that ends before the pre-period. Tested statistic: the change in mean abnormal behaviour from the pre-window to the post-window. p from the mean and spread of the same statistic over 3,000–4,000 draws of pseudo-spikes placed away from real ones (normal approximation; the empirical placebo p is shown as a check). Fewer than 5 spikes: shown, not tested.
      • (c) Distributed lag (a Granger-causality test, which tests timing, not cause; Granger 1969, Econometrica 37(3): 424–438). OLS of behaviour on current and lagged rhetoric plus lagged behaviour; Newey–West (Bartlett) covariance with bandwidth ⌊4(n/100)2/9⌋; F test that all lagged rhetoric coefficients are zero. Check: the same F under circular shifts of rhetoric.
      • Short series. For pairs with fewer than 150 periods (and event studies with fewer than 10 spikes) the p entering the correction is the larger of the model p and the permutation or placebo p, because the model p is unreliable there.
      • Multiple testing. Benjamini–Hochberg over the three primary tests of every pair in a resolution and window. Behaviour-leads and same-period correlations are shown without correction, as context.
      • Calibration. On simulated unrelated series (independent AR(1) pairs, 150–400 per case; scripts/calibrate_null.py, output in scripts/calibration_null.txt), nominal 5% tests rejected about 3% of the time for the lead-lag test (5.5% for its permutation check) and 7.5–12% for the distributed-lag model (4.5% for its permutation check). The distributed-lag model p is liberal, so a distributed-lag result whose permutation check fails should be read as fragile. The event-study rate of 6–8% comes from the original build and has no shipped script.
      • Text policy. Official sentences are quoted up to 300 characters with a link. State media and other media show only the headline and a link.

      Rhetoric coverage

      Gaps: the Russian "recent" collection regimes (RIA, RT, TASS) start in July 2026 and are too short to test; the TASS English "section" sample ends in July 2026, when its "recent" regime begins. The Russian Defence Ministry stream ends in March 2025. The Press TV stream is a "cdx sample": articles found through the Internet Archive's capture index (CDX), not every article. Iranian presidential texts start April 2025, Supreme Leader texts May 2024 to March 2026, and Press TV November 2025 to July 2026. The Venezuelan Foreign Ministry stream is thin before 2024 (338 texts in 2021, 112 in 2022, 474 in 2023), and its old article links on mppre.gob.ve no longer resolve. ACLED Venezuela data end on 8 September 2024. The Telegram stream is archived copies of two channels, built before the live, forward-filtered collection began in October 2026. Not used: North Korean nuclear tests (no North Korean texts in the corpus; last test 2017), the curated Houthi attack list (10 events, not a census).
