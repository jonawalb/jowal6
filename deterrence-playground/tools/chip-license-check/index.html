About the 19 Nov 2026 rule

BIS's interim final rule "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors" (RIN 0694-AK50) was filed for Federal Register public inspection on 16 Nov 2026 as FR Doc 2026-23529, for publication on 19 Nov 2026; it is effective on publication. It formally rescinds the AI Diffusion framework, adds license requirements for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brazil and Thailand, adds a data center end-use control (§ 744.24) and a foundry/OSAT license requirement (§ 744.23(a)(3)(i)(C)), and removes the Data Center VEU.     What this is

    A step-by-step model of how the Export Administration Regulations treat an advanced computing chip, or a computer built on one, in a given transaction. It computes Total Processing Performance and performance density as defined in ECCN 3A090, places the item in 3A090.a, 3A090.b or neither, then walks the license requirement, license exceptions and license review policy for the chosen destination and end user.

    How the numbers are computed

    • TPP = 2 × MacTOPS × bit length (Technical Note 2 to 3A090.a and .b). Datasheets report 2 × MacTOPS as TOPS or TFLOPS, so TPP = dense TOPS × bit length of the inputs. Where a chip runs several precisions, the highest TPP counts. Rates are dense; sparsity figures do not count.
    • Performance density = TPP ÷ applicable die area in mm², counting all logic dies made on a non-planar (FinFET or GAA) process.
    • 3A090.a: TPP ≥ 4800, or TPP ≥ 1600 and PD ≥ 5.92. 3A090.b: TPP 2400 to under 4800 with PD 1.6 to under 5.92, or TPP ≥ 1600 with PD 3.2 to under 5.92. Note 2: chips not designed or marketed for datacenters with TPP under 4800 are outside both.
    • The arithmetic is unit-tested against the two worked examples in the Technical Note (600 TOPS at 8 bits and 300 TFLOPS at 16 bits both give TPP 4800) and against each threshold edge; the decision walk is tested case by case (scripts/model.test.mjs, 196 tests).
    • This tool treats a datasheet's whole-chip peak for a precision as the aggregate over processing units. If a chip has separate units whose peaks add, enter their sum.

    Written rule versus enforced rule

    The regulation text still contains the January 2025 AI Diffusion framework: a worldwide license requirement for 3A090.a, country TPP allocations, and new license exceptions. BIS said on 13 May 2025 that it would not enforce that rule, and in the 14 July 2026 UAE rule stated that it enforces the 3A090.a requirement only for D:1, D:4 and D:5 destinations not also in A:5 or A:6, the UAE, and entities headquartered in D:5 or Macau. The tool shows both: where the text requires a license that BIS does not enforce, the outcome is "No license enforced". GAO held in May 2026 that the non-enforcement announcement is itself a rule under the Congressional Review Act. The formal rescission, with a replacement framework (RIN 0694-AK50, FR Doc 2026-23529), is effective 19 Nov 2026; use the rule-set switch to compare the two texts.

    What it does not model

    • Semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and the .z paragraphs of other ECCNs except as they mirror 3A090. For HBM (3A090.c) the tool walks the license requirement and review policy but does not test the conditions of License Exception HBM (§ 740.25).
    • The de minimis calculation itself (choose whether U.S. controlled content is over or under the level), and whether a foreign-made chip meets the product scope of a foreign direct product rule. Choose the origin you have established.
    • Deemed exports; general (non-data-center) VEU authorizations; the AI Diffusion license exceptions (AIA, § 740.27; LPP, § 740.29) and model-weight control (4E091), all removed by RIN 0694-AK50 effective 19 Nov 2026; and License Exception ACM (§ 740.28), which that rule keeps. Under the foundry presumption in Note 1 to 3A090.a, the tool assumes the transistor-count attestations that can overcome it were not made.
    • Under the eCFR text before 19 Nov 2026, remote access to chips through cloud services beyond flagging BIS's May 2025 policy statement.
    • The review policy for D:1 and D:4 destinations outside D:5 under the text before 19 Nov 2026 while the Diffusion rule is unenforced; it is flagged as unsettled. (3A090.b items reaching review follow the same § 742.6(b)(10)(iii)(A)(1) policy as .a items, but in this model they qualify for NAC or ACA first unless a part 744 end use applies.) Where a part 744 end-use license and a § 742.6(b)(10)(iii) license apply together, the tool shows the stricter review policy and flags that § 742.6 says its own policy applies.
    • Under RIN 0694-AK50: the "is informed" process in § 744.24(a)(2), and how to aggregate TPP across linked data centers.
    • Country embargo programs other than Russia and Belarus (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria), and OFAC sanctions.

    RIN 0694-AK50, effective 19 November 2026

    The RIN 0694-AK50 setting applies the regulatory text filed for public inspection as FR Doc 2026-23529 on 16 Nov 2026 and effective on its publication date, 19 Nov 2026 ("This rule is effective [INSERT DATE OF PUBLICATION IN THE FEDERAL REGISTER]"; the Federal Register lists publication for 19 Nov 2026). In this mode: the worldwide Diffusion requirement, country allocations, AIA, LPP and ECCN 4E091 are gone; one license list covers all 3A090 (except .c), 4A090 and .z items (Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brazil, Thailand; D:1, D:4, D:5 not in A:5/A:6; the UAE except supp. 8 recipients), with a U.S.-headquartered recipient exclusion for the five new countries when the exporter reports under § 743.10; review is case-by-case for the five, presumption of approval for D:1, D:4 and the UAE, presumption of denial for D:5, Macau and D:5-headquartered entities except the existing China/Macau case-by-case path; § 744.24 requires a license for items going to a data center that gives D:5- or Macau-headquartered companies more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP, and de minimis cannot be relied on for that end use; § 744.23(a)(3)(i)(C) requires front-end fabricators and OSATs to obtain a license for 3A090.a chips from designers that are neither approved nor authorized; and the Data Center VEU is removed. The outcome box states whether the result differs from the earlier text.

    Presets

    Chip figures come from vendor product pages and whitepapers, linked under each preset. Where a vendor prints only a figure "with sparsity", the dense value is half, matching the dense/sparse pairs the same vendor prints elsewhere; each preset says so. NVIDIA publishes no H20 figures and Huawei none for the Ascend 910B/C/D, so those presets carry no numbers and rely on the sources that address them directly.
