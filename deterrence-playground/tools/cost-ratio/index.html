Cheap drones and missiles change who pays for a war more than how much the war costs. Hold the total cost fixed, shift it between a strong and a weak actor, and watch where the peaceful settlement lands.

How to read it

The tool implements the formal model in Jonathan Walberg’s working paper Cheap Precision and the Bargaining Range: A Cost-Ratio Extension of Fearon with Scope Conditions for Weak-Actor Leverage. A strong actor S and a weak actor W dispute a good worth 1. S proposes a split; W accepts or goes to war. The paper’s addition is the cost-exchange ratio κ, S’s cost of war divided by W’s, with the total cost of war held fixed.

  • Where the deal lands lays the bargaining range on the line from 0 to 1. Its left half is W’s war cost and its right half is S’s, split at p. The diamond is the offer S makes and W accepts.
  • Proposition 3 plots W’s share against κ on a log scale. Left of 1, S’s costs are lower; right of 1, W’s are.
  • Proposition 4 compares one dyad fought as a denial war and as a survival war.
  • The κ index is the paper’s plan for measuring κ in real conflicts (Section 5).
  • The side panel states the outcome, explains your last change, and substitutes the current numbers into each proposition.
  • The address bar stores every parameter, so a link reproduces the exact setup.

Questions to try

  1. Move κ from 0.5 to 2. How much wider does the bargaining range get, and why?
  2. Raise C with κ below 1, then with κ above 1. Who absorbs most of the extra cost in each case?
  3. Why can W never get more than p, however high κ goes?

Method · The game

Moves. S proposes \(x\in[0,1]\), W’s share of the good. W accepts, for payoffs \((x,\,1-x)\), or fights, for \((p-c_W,\;1-p-c_S)\), where \(p\) is the probability W wins. Information is complete; the solution concept is subgame perfection.

Cost technology. Total war cost \(C=c_W+c_S\) is fixed, and the cost-exchange ratio is \(\kappa=c_S/c_W\). Then

$$c_W=\frac{C}{1+\kappa},\qquad c_S=\frac{\kappa C}{1+\kappa}.$$

\(p\) says who would win a war; \(\kappa\) says who pays for it. At \(\kappa=1\) the costs are equal.

Method · Propositions

1. Bargaining range. \(\mathcal B(\kappa)=\big[p-\tfrac{C}{1+\kappa},\;p+\tfrac{\kappa C}{1+\kappa}\big]\), of width \(C\) for every \(\kappa\).

2. Equilibrium. S offers \(x^*(\kappa)=p-\tfrac{C}{1+\kappa}\) and W accepts. S keeps \(1-x^*\).

3. Comparative static. \(\partial x^*/\partial\kappa=C/(1+\kappa)^2>0\). W gets more than the benchmark \(p-C/2\) when \(\kappa>1\) and less when \(\kappa<1\); its share approaches \(p\) as \(\kappa\) grows.

4. Scope condition. Let \(\tau\in\{D,V\}\) be denial or survival and suppose \(\kappa_D>1>\kappa_V\). For fixed \(p\) and \(C\),

$$x^*_D-x^*_V=\frac{C(\kappa_D-\kappa_V)}{(1+\kappa_D)(1+\kappa_V)}>0.$$

The paper traces the sign of \(\kappa\) to target geometry. In a denial war W strikes S’s spread-out, mobile, expensive forces; in a survival war S strikes W’s few, fixed leadership and command nodes.

Method · Measurement and scope

The κ index. Section 5 scores three parts on \([0,1]\): offensive cost asymmetry, defensive cost burden, and target exposure (coded so that higher means less exposed), and averages them with equal weights. An index above 0.5 marks the high-κ regime. The paper claims only that the index orders cases on κ correctly.

Planned test. An ordered probit of a 0–3 bargaining outcome on the κ index, τ and their interaction, with controls for relative capability, external support and conflict type, on 30 to 50 conflict phases since 1965. The paper predicts positive coefficients on κ, τ and the interaction. The data set is not yet built.

What the tool does and leaves out. Sliders keep \(C\le\min(p,1-p)\) and \(0.1\le\kappa\le10\), so both ends of the range stay inside \([0,1]\): the interior case the propositions describe. The paper’s section on uncertainty about κ is a sketch with the derivation left to an appendix, so it is not built here. Every parameter value is notional, and case cards place each case at a notional κ on the side of 1 the paper codes.
