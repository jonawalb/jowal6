Suppress, Neutralize, Destroy

A call for fire is a standardized request for the fire needed on a target. Suppression keeps the enemy’s heads down only while the rounds keep coming; neutralization takes the target out of action for a short time; destruction takes it out for a long time or for good. In this model each step costs more rounds and more minutes. This figure shows the trade.

This section follows the view you are on. Switch views with the buttons under the card or the V key; the same model, the same mission and the same readout drive both. Reset puts the figure back where it started.

How to read it: the machine

What is on the bench

  1. The target is the lit block. It sits on a spring between four guide posts, so it pops back up whenever nothing holds it down.
  2. The press stands over it: two columns, a crown, a lead screw and a handwheel, and a ram with a pad that drives the block down. The handwheel turns while rounds are being fired.
  3. The rack beside the near column has three detent teeth, from the top: suppress, neutralize and destroy. The stop pin marks the detent chosen, and a pawl on the ram can lock into the rack.
  4. The dials count rounds fired (left) and minutes since the first round (right; the mission window is eight minutes).

How it moves

  1. Pick the effect with the three buttons or the keys 1, 2 and 3. That sets the stop pin.
  2. Fire for effect (the button, a click on the figure or F). The ram comes down onto the block at once and goes deeper as rounds add up, to the chosen detent. For neutralize and destroy the press stops there by itself: end of mission.
  3. Suppress works differently: once the ram reaches the first detent it keeps holding, and the round counter keeps climbing at a slower rate, for as long as you keep firing. Cease fire and the block springs straight back up.
  4. Neutralize: when the rounds stop, the pawl locks the ram for a short time, then lets go and the block comes back up. Destroy: the pawl stays locked and the block cracks; it does not come back.

How to read it: the battlefield

Here the scale is a platoon fight, blue is friendly and red is the enemy, and every round comes from the company’s mortar section, off the map to the rear.

What is on the ground

  1. The enemy is a rifle platoon dug into two-man fighting positions on the rise, with a machine-gun nest at the center of its front and a belt of wire on the slope. The red fan on the ground is the machine gun’s sector; it fades as that fire dies away.
  2. The lit friendly platoon (two rifle squads, the platoon leader and his radio operator) waits at the edge of the wood on the left. Its job is to cross the open ground to the farm on the right, marked in chalk on pegs, and the crossing takes eight minutes. Each squad is “two fire teams and a squad leader” (ATP 3-21.8, para. 1-9), moving in squad column with its teams in wedges, the wedge being “the basic formation of the fire team” (para. 3-46).
  3. The forward observer and his radio operator kneel on the knoll to the right, where they can see the enemy position. Company fire support teams “enable effective artillery, mortar, and joint FS for the maneuver force” (FM 3-09, para. 2-40).

How it moves

  1. Pick the effect with the three buttons or the keys 1, 2 and 3, then Fire for effect (the button, a click on the field or F). Rounds burst on the enemy position and the platoon starts across.
  2. While rounds land the enemy keeps its heads down and fires little. The bursts drawn are a sample, not one per round.
  3. Suppress keeps the rounds coming, at a slower rate, until you cease fire or the platoon is across. Cease fire early and the enemy is straight back up, firing on the platoon in the open; the squads then bound, one firing from the ground while the other moves.
  4. Neutralize stops by itself once its rounds are fired; the enemy stays down for a short time, then comes back with fewer men. Destroy takes three times as many rounds again, and most of the platoon’s eight minutes, but the enemy does not come back.
  5. Losses are notional: greyed figures lying still are men down. The enemy’s lie behind their holes; the platoon’s are carried back to a collection point at the edge of the wood, where a medic kneels. The readout counts them, friendly vs enemy.

Soldiers are drawn larger than scale (about one and a half times) so they read; sizes, ranges, rates of fire, timings, losses and the spacing of the positions are notional.

What doctrine says

U.S. Army FM 3-09, Fire Support and Field Artillery Operations (12 August 2024) defines the three effects “in the context of the computed effects of field artillery fires” (para. 3-66):

EffectFM 3-09, para. 3-66Casualties or materiel damageIn this model (notional)
Suppression“renders a target ineffective for a short period of time”“at least 3-percent”6 rounds, 0.6 min, then 4 rounds a minute to keep it going
Neutralization“renders a target ineffective for a short period of time”“10-percent”18 rounds, 1.8 min
Destruction“renders a target out of action permanently, or ineffective for a long period of time”“30-percent”61 rounds, 6.1 min

The same paragraph defines suppressive fire as “fires on or about a weapons system to degrade its performance below the level needed to fulfill its mission objectives during the conduct of the fires.” That last phrase is why the press must keep holding: suppression lasts while the fire lasts. For the mortars themselves, the manual describes them as “high-angle, relatively short-range, high rate-of-fire, area fire weapons,” organized as sections in infantry brigade combat team rifle companies (para. 2-88).

What is notional

Only the three percentages are doctrine. Everything else is a teaching value: the share of the enemy hit after n rounds fired for effect is 1 − e−n/170, which reaches 3% at 6 rounds, 10% at 18 and 30% at 61; the section fires 10 rounds a minute for effect and 4 a minute to sustain a suppression (sustaining rounds keep heads down but, in this model, add no casualties); a neutralized enemy stays down for 2 minutes; while rounds land, 15% of the enemy’s fire still comes back. The enemy platoon is 26 men and the crossing takes 8 minutes. The platoon’s men down are 0.6 per minute of the enemy’s full fire during the crossing, so in this model ceasing a suppression after 2 minutes costs about 4 men, holding it the whole way about 1 man for 35 rounds, neutralizing 2, and destroying 1 for 61 rounds.

Sources: Headquarters, Department of the Army, FM 3-09, Fire Support and Field Artillery Operations, 12 August 2024, paras. 2-40, 2-88 and 3-66. Headquarters, Department of the Army, ATP 3-21.8, Infantry Rifle Platoon and Squad, 11 January 2024, incorporating Change 1, June 2025 (archived copy), paras. 1-9 and 3-46.
