Maneuver Trainer: Medium

Turn by turn. Read the briefing, then give each of your elements an order: where to go and how, what to shoot at, when to assault. End the turn and the battle runs for a short stretch of game time for both sides at once, then stops so you can see what changed and give new orders. Orders stand until you change them.

Pick a scenario

    How to play

    1. Briefing. Mission, enemy, terrain, troops and time, as an order would give them. Your troops are blue; the enemy appears in red only once one of your soldiers has seen it, and a faint red chalk ring marks where it was last seen.
    2. Orders. Select an element: tap it on the map or in the roster, or press its number key. Pick an order, then tap the ground: where to move (tap near a dashed chalk route to follow it, for example a ditch or a stream bed), where to set a base of fire and what to fire at, which enemy to assault. New orders are drawn as black chalk arrows, standing orders in white. An element keeps its order until you give it a new one.
    3. End the turn. Press End turn (or the space bar). Both sides act at once for one turn of game time: a notional 20 seconds for a fire team, 45 seconds for a platoon, 2 minutes for a company. Then the clock stops and the panel shows what changed: who was hit, what new enemy you found, who is pinned down.
    4. Review. When the mission ends, the score ranks the objective first, then your losses, then time. The after-action review says what happened (drag the replay), why, and what to sustain or improve, with links to the figures that explain each concept. Retry replays the same run from the start; Show the recommended plan draws it on the map and can fill in each turn’s orders for you.

    Orders

    Move (traveling, traveling overwatch, bounding overwatch, slow concealed movement or rush, in the formations that fit the element), Bypass, Support by fire (set a base of fire and suppress a target; when you lift it, it stops firing and stays), Assault, Hold / defend (dig in over time), Reserve, Counterattack, Breach (an obstacle; needs the defenders suppressed), Break contact (alternating bounds or everyone at once), Observe / reconnoiter, Smoke, Call for fire, Shift fire and Lift fire. Bounding overwatch is a technique for moving when not in contact; once fired on, an element uses fire and movement.

    Keys: 1–9 select an element, Esc deselects or cancels, space ends the turn.

    What is notional

    The scenarios, places and enemy are fictional. Every number (ranges, rates of fire, suppression, casualties, times) is a notional teaching value chosen so the lesson of each scenario shows, not data and not a model of real weapons. Casualties fall on both sides even when a plan works; soldiers who are down are shown greyed and still, and are carried back to a casualty collection point. Soldiers, trees and positions are drawn larger than scale so they read. This is not a US Army training aid.

    Terms follow Headquarters, Department of the Army, ATP 3-21.8, Infantry Rifle Platoon and Squad (11 January 2024, incorporating Change 1, June 2025): the fire team (team leader, automatic rifleman, grenadier, rifleman, para. 1-11), the squad as two fire teams and a squad leader (para. 1-9), fire and movement in contact (para. 1-9), support by fire (para. 3-56); and FM 3-90, Tactics (1 May 2023) for the forms of maneuver (para. 2-24). Map drawn with the open-source Anatomy isometric kit (MIT).
