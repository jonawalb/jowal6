How to read it

Each colored band is one country's estimated stockpile: the warheads assigned to its military forces, deployed or in storage. The bands stack, so the top edge of the colored area is the sum of the countries you have switched on. The dashed line is the global total inventory published with the same series, which also counts retired warheads that are intact and waiting to be dismantled. The gap between the stack and the dashed line is mostly those retired warheads.

Switch to Lines to compare countries directly, and turn on Log scale to read the arsenals of the smaller nuclear powers next to the U.S. and Russian ones. Every number is an estimate. Most governments publish nothing about their stockpiles, and the estimates for Israel, India, Pakistan and North Korea are the least certain.

The milestone markers are not a claim that a treaty caused the change in the curve. SALT I limited missiles while both stockpiles kept growing, and FAS says the overwhelming portion of the post-Cold War reduction happened in the 1990s.

Method

  • Annual stockpiles, 1945–2026, for the nine nuclear-armed states and South Africa come from Our World in Data's "Number of nuclear warheads" indicator, which processes the FAS "Estimated Global Nuclear Warhead Inventories" (Kristensen, Korda, Norris, Johns and Knight-Boyle). OWID dataset version 2026-05-13, FAS published 2026-03-30, retrieved 2026-09-29. Values are used as published, with no smoothing or interpolation.
  • The split into deployed strategic, deployed nonstrategic, reserve and retired warheads exists only for the latest year (2026). No year-by-year status series is published, so the timeline shows stockpiles only.
  • The global line is OWID's "World" series, which includes retired warheads. Before the late 1980s it sits slightly above or below the sum of national stockpiles in some years, because the historical global and national estimates were compiled separately. This tool does not derive a retired-warhead series from that difference.
  • FAS notes that its 2026 table figures may not add up, because of rounding and uncertainty. One example: the table lists India with 178 reserve warheads and a military stockpile of 190. The FAS page headlines about 12,187 warheads worldwide, while one of its footnotes gives 12,321; this tool uses 12,187, which matches the OWID series.
  • Milestone dates come from the treaty records and official statements listed below. The New START status was checked on 2026-09-29.
  • No delivery systems, bases or targeting information appear in this tool.

Sources

  • Our World in Data, Estimated nuclear warhead stockpiles and Estimated nuclear warhead inventories (CSV downloads, CC BY 4.0), based on FAS; retrieved 2026-09-29. Regenerate with scripts/build_data.py.

  • CTBT: State Department treaty record; CTBTO treaty page and status table (archived; ctbto.org blocks automated requests); Kremlin, law revoking Russia's ratification, Nov. 2, 2023.
  • New START: State Department; 2021 extension; Kremlin statements of Feb. 21, 2023, Sept. 22, 2025 and Feb. 4, 2026; Arms Control Association fact sheet and statement of Feb. 4, 2026. The State Department page says the extension kept the treaty in force "through February 4, 2026"; the 2021 extension statement and the Kremlin give February 5.
