Analysis, not legal advice

What this implies for AI-generated text

No court ruling on whether model outputs are "technical data" or "published" information is cited here, and none was found in the sources reviewed. The points below are a reading of the definitions, not settled law.

  • The tests are about content and release, not authorship. The ITAR and EAR definitions turn on what information enables and how it is made available. Nothing in them exempts text because software wrote it.
  • A tailored answer is not obviously "public domain". 22 CFR 120.34 lists specific channels of publication. A response generated for one user does not clearly pass through any of them, even if each fact in it appears somewhere public.
  • Machine-ready output gets special treatment. 734.7(c) keeps firearm production files under the EAR even when posted publicly. Regulators have already drawn a line at executable files, separate from descriptive text.
  • Interaction can look like assistance. The "defense service" definition covers assistance and training to foreign persons. Whether interactive help from a model could ever fit is untested.
  • The legal floor is not the policy line. Export law sets minimums for transfers. A content policy can, and usually does, decline operational help that would be lawful to publish.

What this is

A structured index of 20 legal and policy instruments and 16 item types. Each link between an item and a regime records the controlling provision, a one-line plain reading, a coverage level, and a URL to the text it rests on. No data here is synthetic.

How it was built

  • US regulations were read from the eCFR (text current through 2 Oct 2026), statutes from the US Code via Cornell LII (uscode.house.gov was down for maintenance on 6 Oct 2026), and rule histories from the Federal Register API.
  • Treaty dates, party counts and withdrawals come from the UN Treaty Collection status pages as at 6 Oct 2026; Wassenaar and MTCR entries from their official sites.
  • Court holdings were read from the opinions where available (Supreme Court, Fifth and Ninth Circuits). One 2026 district court ruling is cited from a secondary source and flagged as such.
  • EUR-Lex was not fully available on 6 Oct 2026. EU entries were checked through European Commission pages, a Council document and the legislation.gov.uk archive of EU instruments.
  • DoD Directive 3000.09 blocks automated retrieval; its content was checked through the Congressional Research Service primer IF11150 (updated 26 Mar 2026).
  • Claims that could not be verified were left out. The data files are regimes.json, items.json and timeline.json.

Limits

  • Coverage levels are judgments about how a class of item maps to an instrument. They are not commodity jurisdiction or classification determinations.
  • Item types are generic. The tool does not classify any real product and gives no technical parameters beyond those printed in the cited texts.
  • National implementation of multilateral regimes varies. State law, sanctions and embargo regimes are out of scope.
  • Rules change. Each regime shows the date it was last checked.

How to cite
