# Encirclement and Pocket: fact check

UNVERIFIED: pending independent check

Source text read this session from a local copy of FM 3-90 (1 May 2023) PDF, text extraction (title page: "FM 3-90 Tactics MAY 2023 … Washington, D.C., 01 May 2023"), Appendix C "Encirclements". Official URL: https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN38160-FM_3-90-000-WEB-1.pdf · archived copy: https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN38160-FM_3-90-000-WEB-1.pdf

| Claim on the page | Source | Edition / location | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lede: an encirclement cuts an enemy force off from every ground route in or out (paraphrase of "isolate it by controlling all ground lines of communications and reinforcement") | HQDA, FM 3-90, *Tactics* | 1 May 2023, para. C-1, p. C-1 | official / archived above |
| Lede and text: encirclements "usually result from the linkup of two encircling arms conducting a double envelopment" | same | para. C-2, p. C-1 | same |
| The unit organizes encircling forces into "a direct pressure force and one or more encircling arms" | same | para. C-3, p. C-1 | same |
| Block quote and card caption: "The encircling force completes the encirclement when it cuts all enemy ground LOCs. This generally occurs when the two arms of a double envelopment complete their linkup." ("generally" kept) | same | para. C-14, p. C-4 | same |
| "An encircled enemy force attempts to discover intervals and take advantage of them as it tries to escape or breakout of the encirclement"; once linkup is made the force "must close these intervals as quickly as possible" | same | para. C-15, p. C-4 | same |
| FM 3-90 lists "four techniques for reducing an encircled enemy": squeeze, hammer and anvil, wedge and escape route | same | para. C-21, p. C-5 | same |
| Squeeze: "units use fire and movement together in a controlled contraction of the encirclement"; it "is effective against battalion-size or smaller groups of encircled enemy forces"; as the perimeter contracts, commanders take units out of the inner perimeter and add them to the reserve, depending on the terrain and other mission variables | same | para. C-22, p. C-5 | same |
| The wedge splits the pocket (paraphrase of "uses a unit to divide enemy forces in the pocket") | same | para. C-25, p. C-6 | same |
| Escape route technique: "leaving one or more gaps in the inner encircling arm to entice enemy forces to attempt a breakout," because an enemy that is moving and no longer sheltered in its positions is more vulnerable | same | para. C-26, p. C-7 | same |
| FM 3-90 also has units fix or contain an encircled force instead of destroying it | same | para. C-6, p. C-2 | same |
| "Ideally, an encirclement results in the surrender of the encircled force. This minimizes friendly force losses and resource expenditures." | same | para. C-2, p. C-1 | same |
| Battlefield view: a chalk contact point at the linkup (FM 3-90 says the unit "may also establish contact points"; cited in METHOD.md, not quoted on the page) | same | para. C-9, p. C-3 | same |

Not factual claims (labeled notional on the page and in METHOD.md): every distance (1.8 km and 0.85 km radius, 0.75 km per company, 0.7 km breakout gap), every time, the thin-ring rule, the fixed order of events, the casualty rules and counts, unit compositions in the Battlefield view (three vehicles per company, a 10-vehicle enemy battalion), and the mapping of parts to forces. No map symbols are drawn, so MIL-STD-2525D is not cited.


VERIFIED 2026-10-14 by an independent fact-checker against the Wayback copy of FM 3-90 (1 May 2023), Appendix C: all 12 claims PASS (quotes exact, qualifiers kept, C-21 lists all four techniques in order, page numbers right, model conclusions marked "in this model"). One fix applied: C-22 quote now reads "[u]nits use fire and movement…" (manual capitalizes "Units").
