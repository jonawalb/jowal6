    What this is

    A working model of the measurement layer behind a content-review operation for weapons policy. It answers three questions a policy or operations lead asks every week: is the queue keeping up, are decisions consistent and correct, and has the classifier changed. All data is synthetic. Items carry metadata only (time, surface, policy area, classifier score, labels). There is no content text of any kind.

    Data

    A seeded Python script (scripts/generate.py, seed 20261006) simulates 26 weeks, 5 January to 5 July 2026, and writes seven Parquet tables (about 1 MB) that DuckDB-WASM loads in the browser.

    TableRowsContents
    items32,765Queue items: arrival time, surface (chat / api / agentic), policy area, classifier score and flag, severity tier, route (flag or QA sample), QA weight, double-review flag, final label and time
    reviews38,228One row per human decision: primary, second (double review) or adjudication; label, time, seconds spent
    appeals837Appeals on items actioned as violating: filed, resolved, outcome
    reviewers2716 tenured, 8 in a new cohort, 3 senior adjudicators
    traffic546All scored traffic by week, surface and area (about 200,000 items), flagged or not
    score_hist520Weekly score histogram of all scored traffic, 0.05 bins
    sla_policy3SLA hours by severity tier

    How the simulation works: traffic arrives with a hidden "true" status that is never exported. The classifier score is drawn from one beta distribution for violating items and another for the rest. Flagged items (score ≥ 0.50) and an 8% QA sample of unflagged items enter the queue. Reviewers work hourly shifts, highest severity first. Each reviewer labels correctly with a probability set by cohort and item difficulty, and may choose escalate. Escalated items and double-review disagreements go to a senior adjudicator, whose label is final. Appeals are much more likely when the action was wrong, and wrong actions are usually overturned.

    Formulas

    Time to decision is final label time minus arrival time. SLA breach: decided after the tier's SLA, or still open and already past it. Open items not yet past SLA are left out, since their outcome is unknown. Escalation rate: share of reviewed items where any reviewer chose escalate.

    Cohen's κ (Cohen 1960), for two ratings per item:

    κ = (p_o − p_e) / (1 − p_e) p_o = share of items where the two labels match p_e = Σ_k p1(k) · p2(k) (chance agreement from each position's label shares)

    Krippendorff's α, nominal, two coders, no missing values (Hayes and Krippendorff 2007):

    α = 1 − D_o / D_e = 1 − (n − 1) · Σ_{c≠k} o_ck / Σ_{c≠k} n_c · n_k o_ck = coincidences of labels c and k across items (each disagreeing item adds 2) n_c = how often label c was used; n = 2 × items

    Both are computed in SQL (open kappa_cohort and alpha_period) and checked against an independent Python implementation in scripts/check.py. Reading guides: Landis and Koch (1977) call κ of 0.61–0.80 "substantial" and 0.81–1.00 "almost perfect". Krippendorff (2004) advises relying on α ≥ 0.800 and drawing only tentative conclusions from 0.667 to 0.800.

    Precision, recall, F1 against the final human label. QA-sample items carry weight 1/0.08 = 12.5, so that the reviewed sample stands in for all unflagged traffic:

    precision = Σw·[flag ∧ violating] / Σw·[flag] recall = Σw·[flag ∧ violating] / Σw·[violating] F1 = 2·TP / (2·TP + FP + FN)

    p-chart. Center line p̄ is the pooled positive rate in weeks 1–8; limits are p̄ ± 3·√(p̄(1 − p̄)/n_t) for week t with n_t scored items (NIST/SEMATECH). Alert rule, chosen for this page: |z| > 3 in one week, or |z| > 2 in two consecutive weeks on the same side.

    Why these numbers matter for scaling enforcement

    • Backlog and SLA show when a surge needs more reviewer hours or a triage change before users feel it.
    • Agreement is the ceiling on label quality. If two trained reviewers disagree on a share of items, the policy text or the training is unclear there, and any classifier trained on those labels inherits the noise.
    • Precision and recall by policy area show where automation can carry more of the load and where people must stay in the loop.
    • Drift monitoring catches a model or traffic change in days instead of through appeals weeks later. Checking precision next to the positive rate tells you which one happened.

    Limits

    • Synthetic. The rates, sizes and effects are plausible choices, not estimates from any real operation.
    • Measured recall here is lower than the classifier's true recall in the simulation, because reviewer errors on the QA sample are weighted 12.5×. Real programmes face the same issue; adjudicated gold sets reduce it.
    • With about 8,000 items a week, a p-chart is very sensitive, and small mix shifts can cross 3σ. Laney's p′ chart (2002) widens the limits for that between-week variation.
    • Time to decision by arrival week leaves out items still open at the close, so the last week is slightly optimistic.
    • Agreement uses three categories including escalate. Treating escalate as missing would raise κ and α.

    The console

    DuckDB-WASM 1.30.0 loads from jsdelivr. The tables live in memory in your tab. The console accepts one read-only statement at a time; after loading, the session turns off external file and network access and locks its settings. Nothing you run leaves your browser.
