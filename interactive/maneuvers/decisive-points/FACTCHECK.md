# Decisive Points: fact check

VERIFIED 2026-10-13 by an independent fact-checker (JP 5-0, 1 Dec 2020, Wayback capture 20220715183645; GL-7, IV-32, IV-33). All quotes are verbatim, and "usually" is kept in the lede. Fixed after the check: the single-action sentence restores the insufficient-capabilities and "may often" conditions; an unsourced gloss was cut; the publisher is now the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

All claims come from one source, opened this session as a Wayback copy of the official PDF: Joint Chiefs of Staff, JP 5-0, *Joint Planning*, 1 December 2020. Official URL https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp5_0.pdf (jcs.mil returns 403 here); archived copy read: https://web.archive.org/web/20220715183645id_/https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/Doctrine/pubs/jp5_0.pdf

| Claim on the page | Source | Edition | Location | URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| JP 5-0 defines a decisive point as “key terrain, key event, critical factor, or function that, when acted upon, enables commanders to gain a marked advantage over an enemy or contribute materially to achieving success” (page text and card caption) | JP 5-0 | 1 December 2020 | Glossary, Part II, p. GL-7 ("decisive point") | as above |
| Its examples range from “a constricted sea lane, a hill, or a geosynchronous orbit” to key events such as “achievement of air, space, or maritime superiority” | JP 5-0 | 1 December 2020 | Ch. IV, para. 6.b.(1), p. IV-32 | as above |
| “Although decisive points usually are not COGs, they are the keys to attacking or protecting them.” (also paraphrased in the lede as “the keys to attacking or protecting” a center of gravity) | JP 5-0 | 1 December 2020 | Ch. IV, para. 6.b.(3), p. IV-33 | as above |
| When a commander cannot affect the enemy’s COGs “rapidly with a single action,” the commander “must selectively focus a series of actions against the enemy’s or adversary’s critical vulnerabilities until the cumulative effects of these actions lead to mission success” (page says “the commander”; JP 5-0 says “the supported JFC”) | JP 5-0 | 1 December 2020 | Ch. IV, para. 6.b.(3), p. IV-33 | as above |
| Lede: commanders usually choose an indirect approach to an enemy’s center of gravity (JP 5-0: “COGs are generally well-protected and not vulnerable to a direct approach. Thus, commanders usually choose an indirect approach.”) | JP 5-0 | 1 December 2020 | Ch. IV, para. 6.c, p. IV-33 | as above |
| Real decisive points also include events and functions | JP 5-0 | 1 December 2020 | Glossary p. GL-7 (“key event ... or function”); para. 6.b.(1), p. IV-32 | as above |

Not doctrinal claims: the 30% base load, the 35% and 15% prop shares, the 100% capacity, the 8 mm drop, the 150% dial scale, the fall and the sag are notional and labeled so on the page. “In this model only the four combinations that include both inner pins bring it down” is a property of those notional numbers (tested in `scripts/model.test.mjs`).

Note for the checker: page numbers come from the PDF's running footers; the definition paragraph begins on p. IV-32 and para. 6.b.(3) is on p. IV-33.
